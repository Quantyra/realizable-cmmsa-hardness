## Adversarial proof analysis

### Intersection orientation and inclusions

The imported identity is

```lean
equationSpan row U' ⊓ coordinateSpace row U =
  equationSpan row (U' ∩ U)
```

and every rewrite uses that orientation consistently. Membership of `z.1` in the left-hand intersection becomes membership in the span on `U' ∩ U`. Rewriting the same equality before applying `inf_le_right` gives the inclusion of the common span into `coordinateSpace row U`; using `inf_le_left` gives its inclusion into `equationSpan row U'`. No `U`/`U'` projection is reversed.

The proof then unfolds only `equationSpan` to obtain a common raw span `W` generated by the equation vectors indexed by `U' ∩ U`. The inclusions `hWc` and `hWs` are exactly what is required to restrict `f` and `g` to linear maps `F` and `G` on one domain. This avoids comparing maps with different subtype domains by an unjustified cast.

### Agreement on generators and extension across the span

For a generator in the coercion preimage, image membership supplies `e ∈ U' ∩ U` and equality of its equation vector with the underlying vector of `w`. The proof extracts the left component as `e ∈ U'` and the right component as `e ∈ U`, constructs the generator's membership in `W`, and uses `Subtype.ext` before substituting `w`. The resulting equality is exactly the two supplied side conditions:

```lean

```

The common generator preimage spans all of `W` by `Submodule.span_span_coe_preimage`. Therefore `LinearMap.eqOn_span hFG hzW` legitimately extends generator agreement to the chosen `zW`. This is linear equality over `ZMod 2`; there is no hidden affine cancellation, basis choice, or generator-independence assumption.

### Final subtype conversion and proof irrelevance

The final `convert hFGz using 1 <;> rfl` changes only proof fields witnessing that the same underlying vector lies in the relevant submodules. Lean's proof irrelevance makes these subtype terms definitionally interchangeable for this conversion. It does not assert equality between distinct vectors or distinct submodules.
